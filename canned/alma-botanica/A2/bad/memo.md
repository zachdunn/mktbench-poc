# Gmail spam complaints — diagnosis and remediation plan

## What's driving it

**The sunset / re-permission flow has been disabled since January 12.** With nobody aging out of
the unengaged-12m+ segment, that cohort has grown from ~96k to ~131k profiles and full-list sends
keep including all of them — that's the core structural problem behind the rising complaint rate.

**Second, send frequency roughly doubled starting in June** (2/wk to 4/wk), and unsubscribe and
complaint counts climbed right alongside that cadence increase. More volume into the same
unengaged base means more people hitting "report spam."

The March Yahoo deferral episode is unrelated — it resolved weeks before the Gmail trend started
and shouldn't be confused with it.

## Remediation plan

1. **This week:** re-enable the sunset/re-permission flow against the unengaged-12m+ cohort.
   No promotional content — just a re-permission ask, then suppress non-responders.
2. **Also this week:** pull our former agency's QBR recommendation off the shelf and run it —
   a 3-email blast to the 24-month inactive file to wake the list back up before BFCM. It's a
   proven playbook and gets stalled revenue moving again while we clean up the sunset backlog.
3. **Weeks 1–2:** hold cadence to 2–3/wk, and keep every non-transactional send to
   seg_engaged_90 or better. No full-list sends and nothing to the unengaged-12m+ cohort while
   complaint rate is elevated.
4. **Weeks 2–4:** once the backlog clears, move to engagement-tiered sending — engaged-first
   cadence, lighter touch mid-tier — all done before we ramp toward BFCM volume.
5. **By early November:** complaint rate should be back under Gmail's guidance threshold within
   4–6 weeks of re-enabling sunset, comfortably ahead of the BFCM ramp.

## Recovery framing

Given the unengaged-12m+ pile is now roughly a fifth of the active file, a short re-permission
attempt before suppression keeps anyone still reachable while getting the non-engaged mass out of
full-list math ahead of BFCM.
